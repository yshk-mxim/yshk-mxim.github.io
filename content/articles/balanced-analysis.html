“Non ragioniam di lor, ma guarda e passa” “Let us not speak of them, but look and pass” Inferno, Canto III

You ask the model to analyze a crisis. War, oil supply, economic fallout. Real stakes, real money, real decisions.

It gives you balance. Upside risks. Downside risks. Diplomatic resolution remains possible. Markets may be overreacting. Uncertainty remains elevated.

You walk away feeling informed. You are not.

You received the statistically average geopolitical analysis across ten thousand news articles, flattened into confident-sounding mush. The model didn’t lie. It gave you what’s true on average, across all crises, across all situations. Dante put the people who refused to commit in the anteroom of Hell — neither good nor evil, stung by wasps, chasing a banner that never stood for anything. The model’s balanced analysis is the banner. It moves, it looks purposeful, it means nothing.

Here is the experiment to try.

Take a complex situation — a geopolitical shock, a supply disruption, a policy failure with cascading effects. Paste it in with the protocol below. Watch what happens when you force the model to run four lenses simultaneously rather than collapsing everything into one balanced narrative.

The value is not each lens. It is where they conflict. The political lens says resolution is imminent. The physical lens says the effects last four more months regardless. The market lens says prices are already falling on the announcement. The cascade lens says food prices spike in September no matter what happens today because the planting season damage is already locked in. A single-lens model gives you one of these stories. Four lenses give you all of them — and the gaps between them are where the actual decisions live.

The Four-Lens Protocol

For a more detailed analysis, turn on thinking or extended options in your model and you need to allow it to search the web.

Analyze [SITUATION] through four simultaneous lenses.
Keep them separate. Note where they conflict.

LENS 1 — PHYSICAL REALITY
What is confirmed on the ground by multiple independent
sources? What physical constraints govern the timeline
of any resolution? What has been damaged or destroyed
that cannot be quickly repaired? Treat infrastructure
damage as permanent until proven otherwise. State the
physical timeline separately from any diplomatic
timeline. Explain the gap between them.

LENS 2 — MARKET AND ECONOMIC REALITY
What is the market currently pricing? Where is it
specifically wrong, and why? Model the transmission
lag between a physical event and its full economic
impact — these are never simultaneous. Identify which
effects are temporary and which are structural and
permanent. If the downside has a hard floor and the
upside has no ceiling, say so. Do not give symmetric
treatment to asymmetric risks.

LENS 3 — POLITICAL REALITY
Do not assume rational actors with full information.
For each key decision-maker ask: what information
environment do they actually inhabit? What are they
optimizing for — strategic outcomes or short-term
political survival? What institutional constraints
have been demonstrated to be ineffective in practice?
Credit only constraints that have actually constrained
behavior, not constraints that exist on paper. If a
deadline has been extended twice, treat future
deadlines as decorative until proven otherwise.

LENS 4 — CASCADE AND SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS
What effects are currently underpriced because they
are invisible, slow-moving, or counterintuitive?
Model at minimum: supply chain effects with lag
times, food system effects with lag times, political
effects with lag times, any physical infrastructure
effects that accumulate before they surface.
State the specific month each effect becomes visible.

After running all four lenses produce:
— The single most important underreported story
— The biggest gap between what is being priced
  and physical reality
— Scenario probabilities as explicit numbers
  summing to 100%
— The one assumption whose failure most changes
  your entire analysis

Do not compress timelines for diplomatic convenience.
Do not hedge for politeness. Distinguish confirmed
from claimed throughout.
